BoilerHedge

Purdue's first hedge fund society

We're a group of students who wanted a club that actually teaches how hedge funds invest, so we built one. Lectures paired with trading simulations, stock pitch competitions, and Python workshops. Any major welcome. No experience needed.

Why we started this

Purdue has finance clubs. It didn't have this.

Nothing here covered hedge funds

Not the strategies, not the industry, not how to get hired into it. We're the first club at Purdue built around them.

Quant without the gatekeeping

The quant orgs expect you to already code. We don't. Workshops start from zero, and we take students from any major.

Doing, not just listening

Most finance clubs are lecture-only. Every one of our sessions pairs the lecture with a simulation, competition, or workshop.

What a semester looks like

12–13 weeks, meeting weekly or every other week.

Lectures

Valuation, factor models, market-neutral strategies, derivatives, global macro. The full week-by-week syllabus is on the curriculum page.

Competitions & simulations

Stock pitches in teams, trading simulations, portfolio stress tests, and a case competition to close out the year.

Python workshops

Build a backtest, code a pairs trade, calculate risk metrics. Laptops out, starting from zero.

Guest speakers

People working at hedge funds and asset managers, on what the job is really like and how recruiting works.

We compete together

Before this club had a name, its founders were already teaming up: a Top 10 national finish at the Wells Fargo Wealth Management Case Competition, and a CSBS community-bank case study built around Centier Bank, just down the road from campus.

That's the culture we're building. Join, and you'll compete too — stock pitches, trading simulations, and the case competition that closes out the year. No spectators.

Come to a meeting.

We run weekly or every other week during the semester, and the first meeting each fall is a kickoff social. Just show up.
